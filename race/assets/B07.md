# Privacy first (read this)
Never paste student names, ids, health or family details into an AI tool. Use "the student" or initials, and add real details yourself after the draft. Follow your school or district policy on AI use. These prompts are drafting aids, not replacements for your judgement.
# How the prompts are built
Each prompt names a role, situation, tone, length and one constraint. Replace [brackets]. Add a line at the end such as "Keep it under 120 words" if you want it shorter.
# Parent emails
- "Act as a primary school teacher. Write a friendly email to a parent about a student who has been [struggling with homework completion]. Tone: supportive and specific. Include one positive, one concern and one next step. 140 words."
- "Draft an email asking a parent to schedule a conference. Offer three time windows and a short agenda. Warm tone."
- "Write a short email thanking a parent for helping with [class event]."
- "Write an email explaining a change in classroom routine ([change]). Plain language, include a reason and a start date."
- "Draft a note home about [lost items]. Friendly, non-blaming."
- "Write a gentle reminder about missing permission slips due [date]."
- "Write an email to share good news about a student's improvement in [subject], without comparing to other students."
- "Draft a sensitive email about a student's behaviour [type of behaviour], using facts only, no labels, and a plan for next steps."
- "Write a reply to a parent who is upset about a grade. Calm, acknowledge feelings, explain the grading criteria, offer a meeting."
- "Write a reply to a parent asking for extra work. Offer two options and a deadline."
# Progress notes and reports
- "Write a two-sentence progress comment for a student who [strength] and needs to [growth area]. Use positive language and avoid jargon."
- "Rewrite this comment to be clearer and kinder without changing the facts: [paste your comment, with no names]."
- "Generate five variations of a comment for a student who is [reliable, curious, quiet] in [subject]."
- "Summarise these three observations into one paragraph for a report: [observations]."
# Conference prep
- "Create a one-page agenda for a 15-minute parent conference covering strengths, concerns, goals and home support."
- "List five open questions a teacher can ask parents to learn about a student's learning at home."
- "Write a closing script that summarises agreed actions and follow-up date."
# Newsletters and class communication
- "Draft a monthly class newsletter with sections: what we learned, upcoming dates, how to help at home, thank-yous. 250 words. Cheerful tone."
- "Turn these bullet points into a short weekly update: [bullets]."
- "Write a reminder about testing week: sleep, breakfast, calm tone."
# Quick revision prompts
- "Make this email warmer without being unprofessional."
- "Shorten this to under 100 words and keep the call to action."
- "Check this email for tone and flag any phrase that could be read as blaming."
