# The goal of week one
Stabilise, document what you did, and get your first five conversations booked. You are not applying everywhere yet. You are building a base.
# Day 1: Stabilise (60 to 90 minutes)
- Write down the date, your last day, severance terms and any benefits end dates.
- Save copies of reviews, project lists and contact details you are allowed to keep.
- List monthly bills and note which can pause or reduce.
- Tell three trusted people what happened in one sentence each.
# Day 2: Inventory your wins
- List 10 results from your last two roles. For each write: what you did, what changed, and a number (time saved, money, volume, speed).
- Pick the 5 you are proudest of. They become your bullets.
# Day 3: Resume rewrite
Use the bullet formulas below. Keep one page for under 10 years of experience.
# Day 4: Message ten people
Use the scripts below. Aim for ten short messages: five former colleagues, three recruiters, two people in target companies.
# Day 5: Pick your target list
- Choose 15 companies, not 150.
- For each note one person you could contact and one recent thing about the company.
# Day 6: Practise
- Record yourself answering the five interview questions below.
- Fix the answer that took longest.
# Day 7: Review and rest
- Count conversations booked.
- Set next week's goal: five applications to roles where you already know someone, plus three coffee chats.
# 10 resume bullet formulas
Use the pattern: Verb + what + how many/how much + result.
- "Reduced [process] time from [X] to [Y] by [method]."
- "Led a team of [N] to deliver [project] [N] weeks ahead of schedule."
- "Grew [metric] by [N percent] over [period] by [action]."
- "Trained [N] people on [skill], cutting errors by [N percent]."
- "Managed a budget of $[X] and saved $[Y] by renegotiating [vendor]."
- "Handled [N] customer cases per week with a [N percent] satisfaction rating."
- "Created a [document/tool] now used by [N] people."
- "Resolved [problem] affecting [N] customers within [time]."
- "Introduced a weekly review that improved [metric] by [N percent]."
- "Launched [initiative] reaching [N] people in [time]."
- Swap the opening verb among: built, streamlined, negotiated, mentored, audited, redesigned, coordinated, prioritised, documented, automated.
# 5 email scripts
- To a former colleague: "Hi [Name], I hope you are well. My role at [Company] ended this month and I am looking for [target role]. Would you be open to a 15-minute call, or to pointing me at one person I should speak to? Happy to return the favour any time."
- To a recruiter: "Hi [Name], I am a [role] with [N] years in [field], recently available. My best results include [one number-based win]. If you have roles in [area], I would love to be considered. Resume attached."
- To a hiring manager: "Hi [Name], I saw the [role] posting. In my last role I [result with number], which seems relevant to [need from the posting]. Would a short call make sense?"
- Follow-up after no reply: "Hi [Name], quick follow-up on my note from [day]. If now is not a good time, no worries. I am glad to send a one-page summary."
- Thank-you after an interview: "Thank you for your time today. I enjoyed the discussion about [topic]. One more thought on [question]: [one sentence]. Please let me know what else would be helpful."
# Interview answer bank
- Tell me about yourself: present role, two proof points, why this role next.
- Why did you leave? "My role was eliminated in a reorganisation. I am proud of [result], and I am looking for [what you want next]."
- Biggest strength: name it, give one example with a number.
- A time you disagreed with a manager: describe the disagreement, your approach, the result.
- Questions for them: "What does success look like in the first 90 days?" "What has changed on the team in the last year?"
