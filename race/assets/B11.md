# How to use this outliner
Find the prompt type that matches your scholarship. Answer the questions in your own words in a note. Then follow the outline. Do not copy example wording. Reviewers read hundreds of essays and notice generic ones.
# Before any prompt
- Read the prompt twice and underline the action words (describe, explain, reflect).
- Check the word limit and the deadline.
- Look at the scholarship sponsor's mission and note two words they use about themselves.
# 1. Leadership
- Questions: When did people look to you for direction? What did you decide? What went wrong or was hard?
- Outline: a specific moment, what you did, what changed, what you learned about leading others.
- Mistake: listing titles instead of describing a moment.
# 2. A challenge you overcame
- Questions: What was hard? What did you try first? Who helped? What would you do differently?
- Outline: the situation in two sentences, your actions, the result, how it changed you.
- Mistake: dwelling on the problem and rushing the lesson.
# 3. Community service
- Questions: Why did you start? What did you see that surprised you? What would you improve?
- Outline: why, what you did, one person or moment, what you will keep doing.
- Mistake: hours logged without a story.
# 4. Career goals
- Questions: What problem do you want to work on? What did you do so far? What do you need next?
- Outline: a moment that sparked the goal, current steps, the plan, how the scholarship helps.
- Mistake: vague ambition without proof you have started.
# 5. Financial need
- Questions: What is your situation, in factual terms? What have you done to manage costs? What changes with support?
- Outline: facts, your actions, what the scholarship would change, your commitment.
- Mistake: self-pity or exaggeration. Stay factual and calm.
# 6. A defining moment
- Questions: What day do you remember in detail? What did you notice? What would you tell your younger self?
- Outline: scene, reaction, change, meaning today.
- Mistake: summarising instead of showing the scene.
# 7. A person who influenced you
- Questions: What did they do that nobody else did? What did you copy? What do you now do for others?
- Outline: a short portrait, one story, what you took from them, how you pass it on.
- Mistake: writing only about the person and not yourself.
# 8. Why you deserve this scholarship
- Questions: What have you done with the opportunities you had? What will you do with this one?
- Outline: two or three pieces of proof, a clear plan, a thank-you.
- Mistake: bragging without evidence or comparing yourself to others.
# Final checklist
- Does the first sentence make a reader want the second?
- Does it answer the prompt?
- Is every claim specific?
- Is it under the word limit?
- Read it aloud and fix anything awkward.
- Ask a teacher or friend to read it and say one thing they remember.
- Check spelling of the sponsor's name.
