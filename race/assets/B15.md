# How to use this kit
Pick the headline formula and About structure that match your goal, fill in the brackets with your own real details, then run the final edit pass. Keep every claim something you can back up.
# Headline: why it matters
Your headline shows next to your name in search results, comments and messages. It is the line most people read. Say what you do and who you help, not just your job title.
# 8 headline formulas
- [Role] helping [audience] [result]
- [Role] | [specialty] | [specialty] | [specialty]
- [Role] at [Company]: [one concrete thing you work on]
- [Role] who [verb] [what], for [audience]
- Open to [target role] | [skill] | [skill] | [skill] (for job seekers)
- [Specialty] for [industry]: [outcome] in [timeframe]
- Former [role], now [new role]: [bridge skill]
- [Role] | [credential or proof point, such as a number or notable client type you are allowed to name]
# About section: the three-part shape
Open with the point, not the biography. Most readers see only the first two or three lines before "see more".
- Part 1, the hook (1 to 2 sentences): who you help and with what.
- Part 2, the proof (3 to 5 sentences): what you have done, with numbers or scale where you can.
- Part 3, the next step (1 sentence): what you want the reader to do.
# Structure A: job seeker
"I help [type of team] [do what]. Over [N] years in [field] I have [result with a number], [result], and [result]. I am looking for [target role] at [type of company]. If that sounds like a fit, message me or see my work below."
Worked example (illustrative): "I help support teams answer customers faster without losing the human touch. Over six years in customer support I have cut average response time from 14 hours to 3, built a help centre that deflects about a third of billing tickets, and trained eleven new hires. I am looking for a support operations role at a growing software company. If that sounds like a fit, message me."
# Structure B: freelancer
"I help [audience] [result] through [service]. Recent work includes [project type] for [type of client] that [result]. I work best with [kind of client] who [trait]. To start, [how to contact you or what to send]."
Worked example (illustrative): "I help small online shops turn product pages into sales through clearer copy and tighter structure. Recent work includes rewriting 40 listings for a home-goods shop. I work best with owners who already have steady traffic. To start, send me your best-selling page and what you want it to do."
# Structure C: career changer
"After [N] years in [old field], I moved into [new field] because [honest reason]. What carries over: [skill], [skill], [skill]. What I have built since: [project or course with a concrete output]. I am looking for [role] where I can [contribution]."
Worked example (illustrative): "After eight years teaching high school maths, I moved into data analysis because I enjoyed explaining numbers more than grading them. What carries over: clear explanation, patience with beginners, and planning around deadlines. Since then I have built three dashboards for a local nonprofit. I am looking for a junior analyst role where I can turn data into plain-language decisions."
# Experience and Featured checklist
- Each role: two to four lines, the biggest result first.
- Use the same verbs and numbers as your resume so nothing contradicts.
- Featured: pin one thing that proves the About section (a link, a document, a post).
- Skills: list the ten that match your target role, and put the best three first.
# Final edit pass
- Read the About aloud. Cut every sentence that would fit anyone.
- Replace adjectives ("passionate", "results-driven") with a fact.
- Check every number is real and defensible.
- Remove anything confidential about an employer or client.
- Ask one person: "What do you think I do after reading this?" If the answer is wrong, rewrite the hook.
