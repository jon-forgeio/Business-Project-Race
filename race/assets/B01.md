# How to use this kit
Pick ONE structure below, answer the prompts in plain words, then read it out loud twice. A good toast is 2 to 3 minutes, about 300 to 400 words. Write it to be heard, not read.
# Step 1: Pick your story (5 minutes)
Answer each in one sentence. The best story is the one you can tell in under 40 seconds.
- A moment when you first saw how this person acts around their partner.
- A small, specific habit of theirs that everyone in the room recognizes.
- A time they surprised you by being kinder or braver than expected.
- Something that was going wrong that turned out fine.
Circle the one that makes you smile while writing it. That is your story. Cut the rest.
# Structure A: Best man / close friend
- Opener: one line that gets a laugh or a warm silence (see openers below).
- Who you are: your name and how you know the person, in one sentence.
- The story: your circled story from Step 1, told with one concrete detail (a place, an object, a phrase).
- The turn: "And that is when I knew they would be great at marriage because..." one sentence tying the story to the partnership.
- The partner: two sentences welcoming the partner, mention one thing you noticed about how the two of them fit together.
- The close: raise your glass using a closer below.
# Structure B: Maid or matron of honor
- Opener: a line about the first time you heard about the partner.
- The friendship: one sentence on what the friendship has meant, no inside jokes the room cannot follow.
- The story: your circled story, with the feeling you had in the moment.
- The turn: what you learned about love from watching them.
- Wish: one specific wish for their marriage (a habit, not a cliche), for example "I hope you always make each other laugh on the worst Tuesday of the year."
- The close: raise your glass.
# Structure C: Parent of the couple
- Welcome: thank guests for travelling or showing up.
- A memory from childhood in two sentences. Keep it short.
- What you see now: the person they became.
- Welcome the new family member by name and say one thing you appreciate about them.
- A wish and a toast.
# Structure D: Friend or relative (short, 90 seconds)
- One sentence on who you are.
- One quality you admire in each of them.
- One wish.
- Toast.
# Openers (steal and adapt)
- "For those who do not know me, I am the person who knows too much and has promised to say too little."
- "I was asked to say a few words, which is dangerous because I was also given a microphone."
- "[Name] once told me they would never get married. I am delighted to report they were wrong."
- "I have known [Name] for [X] years, and I have never seen them this happy, this relaxed, or this well dressed."
- "Marriage is a long conversation. Tonight we are celebrating two people who never run out of things to say."
- "I prepared three speeches: a funny one, a sentimental one, and a short one. You are getting a combination."
- "Standing here, I realize I have been practicing this speech in the shower for months. Thank you for being a patient audience."
- "Love is not about finding the perfect person. It is about finding the person whose imperfections match yours."
- "When [Name] introduced me to [Partner], the first thing I noticed was that [Name] listened."
- "A good wedding is about two things: love and food. We have clearly nailed both."
- Opener patterns: start with a number ("Seven years ago..."), start with a quote from the couple, start with a question ("Has anyone here ever seen [Name] be on time?"), start with a gratitude line, start with a very short story.
# Closers
- "Please raise your glasses to [Name] and [Name]: may your home be full of laughter and your fridge be full of leftovers."
- "To the couple: may you always be each other's favorite person to come home to."
- "To [Name] and [Name]. Cheers."
- "To love that is patient, to jokes that are repeated, and to a lifetime of good company."
- "May your best day be tomorrow, and the day after that."
- "To the two of you: thank you for letting us be part of this day."
- Closer patterns: end on a wish for health, end on a wish for travel, end on a callback to your opener, end on a one-word toast ("Cheers"), end with a line about the future.
# Delivery checklist
- Time yourself. Cut anything past 3 minutes.
- Print it in large type or put it on a card. Phones in a bright room are hard to read.
- Do not drink much before speaking.
- Breathe, pause after jokes, look at the couple for the last line.
- Avoid: exes, money, embarrassing secrets, anything that needs an explanation.
- Practice twice out loud the day before.
# Last check
Ask a friend: Did the story make you smile? Was there anything you would not say in front of the couple's grandparents? If yes, cut it.
