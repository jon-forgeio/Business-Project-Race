# A Hosting Checklist for a Weekend Visit

Having people to stay for a weekend is lovely, and it can also turn into two days of running around looking for spare pillows. The difference between a relaxed weekend and a frantic one is usually a bit of preparation spread over the week before.

Here is a checklist you can copy into your notes and work through. It assumes a two-night stay with friends or family, but it scales up or down easily.

## A week before: ask and decide

A few questions now save a lot of guessing later.

- Confirm arrival and departure times.
- Ask about food: allergies, things they do not eat, and anything they love.
- Ask if they have plans of their own, or if they would like you to plan things.
- Check how they are travelling and whether they need picking up or a parking spot.
- Decide where everyone will sleep, and check you have enough bedding.

Write the answers in one note. You will refer to it all weekend.

## A few days before: plan the meals

You do not need a menu, just a rough idea.

- **Friday evening:** something easy that can wait if they arrive late. A soup, a traybake, or a takeaway plan.
- **Saturday breakfast:** simple and self-serve, such as bread, fruit, yoghurt and coffee.
- **Saturday lunch:** often out, or leftovers.
- **Saturday dinner:** the one meal worth putting effort into, if you want to.
- **Sunday breakfast:** something a little special but quick.

Then write a shopping list and buy most of it two days ahead.

## Send a welcome message

A short message a couple of days before helps guests arrive relaxed:

> Hi [Name], we are really looking forward to seeing you on [day]. A few things that might help:
> - Address: [address]
> - Parking: [where to park]
> - We will be home from [time]. If you are running late, no problem, just send a message.
> - Wifi details are on the fridge.
> - Bring [anything useful: walking shoes, a swimsuit, a warm layer].
>
> Safe travels, and let us know if you need anything before then.

## The day before: the guest room

Walk into the guest space as if you have never seen it.

- Fresh sheets on the bed, and a spare blanket.
- Clean towels, one large and one small per person, left on the bed.
- Clear space in a drawer or a few hangers in a wardrobe.
- A lamp or bedside light that works.
- A phone charger and a free plug socket nearby.
- A glass or bottle for water.
- A box of tissues.

## The day before: the bathroom

- A spare toothbrush, toothpaste and soap.
- Plenty of toilet paper, where guests can find it without asking.
- Shampoo and shower gel, if your guests might not bring their own.
- A clear spot for their toiletry bag.

## The day before: the rest of the house

- Write the wifi name and password on a card.
- Clear a spot near the door for shoes, coats and bags.
- Show-ready the kitchen: guests should be able to find mugs, tea, coffee and glasses on their own.
- Tidy, but do not deep clean. Nobody is inspecting.

## When they arrive

- Offer a drink straight away.
- Give a short tour: their room, the bathroom, where the light switches are, how the shower works.
- Mention anything unusual, like a door that sticks or a step in the dark.
- Tell them they are welcome to help themselves to tea, coffee and snacks.

That last point matters. Guests often feel awkward asking, so saying it once makes the weekend easier for everyone.

## During the weekend

- Leave some unplanned time. Not every hour needs an activity.
- Let guests help if they offer. Washing up together is often where the best conversations happen.
- Keep breakfast flexible. People wake at different times.

## When they leave

- Ask them to strip the bed and leave the sheets in a pile, if you are comfortable asking. Many guests are glad to have a clear task.
- Send a short thank-you message once they are home.
- Write down anything you would do differently next time, and anything you ran out of.
