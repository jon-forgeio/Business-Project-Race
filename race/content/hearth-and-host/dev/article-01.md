# A Six-Week Moving Timeline That Keeps the Last Week Calm

Moving is rarely hard because of any single task. It gets hard because forty small tasks land in the same five days. The fix is not working harder in the final week. It is moving most of those tasks earlier, while they are still small.

Below is a six-week plan you can paste into any notes or task app. Adjust the weeks to your own date, and check local rules for anything official, such as parking permits for a moving truck or notice periods on a lease.

## Six weeks out: decide and book

This week is about decisions, not boxes.

- Confirm your moving date and write it at the top of your list.
- Decide how you are moving: a moving company, a rented van with friends, or a mix.
- Ask for quotes or check van availability, and book once you have chosen.
- Start one running note called "Move" with three headings: To do, To buy, To tell.
- Walk through each room and list anything you will not take. Sell, donate or give it away now, while you have time to do it properly.

## Five weeks out: start with what you will not miss

Pack the things you will not touch for a month and a half.

- Out-of-season clothes, spare bedding, books, decorations, hobby gear.
- Label every box with the room it goes to and a short list of what is inside. "Kitchen: baking tins, cookbooks" is far more useful than "Kitchen".
- Number the boxes. Keep a short list in your Move note so you can tell later if one is missing.
- Gather supplies: boxes, tape, a marker per person, and something soft for wrapping.

## Four weeks out: the paperwork week

This is the week to handle the tasks that need someone else to act.

- List every place that has your address: bank, employer, doctor, dentist, subscriptions, insurance, any memberships, and family who send cards.
- Set up mail forwarding if it is offered where you live.
- Contact your utility providers to arrange the switch-off at the old place and the switch-on at the new one.
- If you have school-age children or pets, check what the new area needs from you (enrolment, registration, a new vet).

## Three weeks out: the slow rooms

Some rooms take longer than you expect. Start them now.

- Pack the spare room, the garage or storage cupboard, and anything in the loft or under the stairs.
- Use up food in the freezer and pantry instead of buying more.
- Book any time off work you need for the move and the day after.
- Confirm details with your movers or helpers: start time, address, parking.

## Two weeks out: most of the house

Aim to have everything packed except what you use every day.

- Pack living room, most of the kitchen, and most of the wardrobe.
- Leave out one plate, bowl, mug and set of cutlery per person.
- Measure large furniture and check doorways at the new place if you can.
- Take a few photos of how your electronics are wired before you unplug them.

## The final week: the open-first box

The final week should feel like tidying, not panicking. Two things make the difference.

**Pack an open-first box for each person.** This box travels with you, not on the truck. Fill it with:

- Phone chargers and a power strip.
- Toilet paper, hand soap and a towel.
- A change of clothes and anything you need for the next morning.
- Kettle or coffee maker, mugs, and a few snacks.
- Basic tools: scissors, a screwdriver, a box cutter.
- Bedding for the first night.

**Plan the last 72 hours on paper.** Three days before, defrost the freezer if it is moving with you. Two days before, pack the last of the kitchen and bathroom. The day before, confirm timings with everyone and charge your phone.

## Moving day

- Do a last walk-through of every room, cupboard and drawer.
- Take meter readings and photos at both properties.
- Keep valuables, documents and medication with you.
- At the new place, make the beds first. Everything else can wait until morning.

## The first week after

- Unpack the kitchen and bathroom first; they make the house feel usable.
- Work through the "To tell" list for anyone you have not updated yet.
- Break down boxes as you go so they do not take over a room.
