# What to Leave Your Pet Sitter: A Practical Handoff Checklist

You know your pet's routine so well that you do not think about it. That is exactly the problem when someone else takes over. The sitter does not know that the cat only eats from the blue bowl, or that the dog pulls hard when he sees a bike.

A good handoff is mostly about writing down the obvious. Here is a checklist you can work through a few days before you leave. It works whether your sitter is a professional, a neighbour or a family member.

This is a practical organising list, not veterinary advice. For anything about your pet's health, ask your vet.

## Part 1: The care sheet

Write a single page, or a single note, that covers your pet's day. Keep it on the fridge and send a copy by message too.

**Basics**

- Pet name, species, breed, age and weight.
- A recent photo, in case your pet gets out.
- Microchip number, if your pet has one.

**Food**

- Type of food and exact amount per meal.
- Meal times.
- Where the food is kept and where it is served.
- Treats allowed, and any foods to keep away from your pet.

**Medication**

- Name, dose and time.
- How your pet takes it (in food, by hand, in a treat).
- Where it is stored.

**Walks and exercise**

- How long, how often, and your usual route.
- Which leash or harness to use.
- Anything that sets your pet off: other dogs, bikes, loud noises, children.

**Behaviour notes**

- Hiding spots, especially for cats.
- Habits like chewing, escaping through doors or guarding food and toys.
- What calms your pet down.

**Sleep and settling**

- Where your pet sleeps.
- Any bedtime routine.

## Part 2: Contacts

Put these at the top of the care sheet, not the bottom.

- Your phone number, and the best way to reach you while away.
- A backup contact who lives nearby and can make decisions.
- Your vet's name, clinic phone and address.
- The nearest emergency vet and its hours.

Then write one sentence that settles the hardest question in advance: what should the sitter do in an emergency, and is there a spending limit you approve? Deciding this calmly now is far better than a rushed phone call later.

## Part 3: The home

The sitter is also looking after your house for a while.

- Door codes, key location and alarm instructions.
- Where leashes, waste bags, litter, towels and cleaning supplies live.
- Which rooms are off-limits for the pet.
- How the heating or cooling works, if your pet needs a certain temperature.
- Bin days, if the sitter will be there when they come around.

## Part 4: The walk-through

If at all possible, meet in person before you leave.

1. Let your pet approach the sitter first.
2. Walk the home together: feeding spot, supplies, exits, anything hazardous.
3. Have the sitter test the key or door code while you are there.
4. Show the walk route if it is not obvious.
5. Agree on dates, visit times and payment in writing, even if it is just a message.

## Part 5: Updates

Agree on how you want to hear from the sitter. A simple pattern works well:

> Visit done at [time]. [Pet] ate [all / some] of breakfast, had a [length] walk, and is now [napping / playing]. Anything to flag: [none / note].

A short, regular update means you are not left wondering, and the sitter knows exactly what you want to hear.

## Part 6: Before you walk out the door

- Fill food and litter supplies for the whole stay, plus a little extra.
- Leave medication clearly labelled.
- Put the care sheet somewhere obvious.
- Send a final "we have left" message so the sitter knows the handoff is complete.
