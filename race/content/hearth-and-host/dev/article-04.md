# Planning the Household Side of the First Month With a Newborn

When people prepare for a new baby, most of the planning goes into the baby: the cot, the car seat, the tiny clothes. The household tends to get less attention. Then the first weeks arrive, and the questions are suddenly about who is cooking, whether there are clean towels, and how to say no to a visitor without feeling rude.

This article is about that household side. It is an organising guide only. Anything about feeding, sleep, health or recovery belongs with your doctor, midwife or health visitor.

## Start before the baby arrives

The best time to plan the first month is a few weeks before it starts.

- **Stock the basics.** Toilet paper, soap, bin bags, laundry detergent, easy snacks and anything you buy every week. Aim to need very little shopping for a while.
- **Cook ahead if you can.** Even a few frozen meals give you breathing room.
- **Write a helpers list.** Names, phone numbers and what each person is good at: cooking, errands, walking the dog, sitting with an older child.
- **Decide your visitor approach together.** If you have a partner, agree on this now, not at the door.

## Week 1: protect rest

The first week is about keeping the house running at the lowest possible effort.

- Meals: frozen meals, simple food, or meals from others.
- Laundry: one load a day, just baby things and towels. Everything else can wait.
- Visitors: only those you really want, and only briefly.
- Rest: take turns. Agree who is "on" and who is "off" for each stretch.

A good rule for this week: if a task can wait a week without causing harm, let it wait.

## Week 2: find a rhythm

- Meals: keep them simple. Lean on helpers if they offer.
- Laundry: still one load a day, adding a few adult clothes.
- Visitors: short visits with a clear end time.
- Household: pick one small daily task, such as running the dishwasher at night.

## Week 3: small additions

- Add a short outing if it feels right, even a walk around the block.
- Do a quick weekly reset (more on that below).
- Restock anything that is running low.

## Week 4: adjust the plan

- Look at what has worked and what has not.
- Decide which helpers you still need and what for.
- Update the routine for the month ahead.

## The weekly 15-minute reset

Once a week, set a timer for 15 minutes and do the following:

1. Clear one surface that has gathered clutter.
2. Check the fridge and freezer, and write a short shopping list.
3. Restock nappies, wipes and household basics.
4. Look at the coming week and note any appointments or visitors.

Fifteen minutes will not make the house tidy. It does stop small problems from building up.

## Scripts that save awkward conversations

Writing these in advance means you can send them when you are tired, without having to find the right words.

**Asking visitors to wait**

> We are so glad you want to meet the baby. We are taking the first couple of weeks quietly and will let you know when we are ready for visitors. Thank you for understanding.

**Setting a short visit**

> We would love to see you on [day]. We are keeping visits to about [time] for now so we can rest. Could you bring [small item] if it is easy?

**Starting a meal train**

> Hi all, a few people have asked how they can help. Meals are the most useful thing right now. If you would like to bring one, here is a sign-up list: [link]. Please leave it at the door; we will message to say thank you.

## A handoff note for a partner or caregiver

When someone else takes over for a few hours, a short note helps them settle in without needing to ask questions:

- What has happened today so far.
- Anything the baby's doctor or midwife has asked you to keep track of.
- Where supplies are kept.
- When you will be back and how to reach you.
