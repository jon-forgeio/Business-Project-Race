# Guest Message Templates for Short-Term Rental Hosts, From Booking to Checkout

If you host guests in a spare room, a cabin or a holiday flat, you probably write the same handful of messages over and over. Each one takes a few minutes, and they always seem to arrive while you are doing something else.

A small set of saved templates fixes that. The goal is not to sound like a robot. It is to have a warm, clear starting point you can personalise in seconds.

Below are templates for each stage of a stay, followed by a simple way to organise them. Replace anything in [brackets].

## Before you start: one property note

Every message pulls from the same facts. Write them down once in a single note:

- Address and a one-line description of how to find the door.
- Parking instructions.
- How guests get in (key box, code, or meeting in person).
- Wifi network name and where the password is shown in the home.
- Check-in and checkout times.
- Quiet hours and your two or three house rules.
- Your phone number for urgent issues.

With this note open, most replies become copy, paste, adjust.

## Stage 1: Booking confirmed

> Hi [Name], thank you for booking! You are all set for [dates]. I will send arrival details a few days before you come. If you have any questions in the meantime, just message me here.

Short is better. The guest wants to know the booking worked and when they will hear from you next.

## Stage 2: A few days before arrival

> Hi [Name], looking forward to having you on [day]. Here is what you need:
> - Address: [address]
> - Parking: [instructions]
> - Getting in: [method]
> - Check-in from [time], checkout by [time]
> - Wifi details are on the card by the [location]
>
> What time do you expect to arrive? It helps me make sure everything is ready.

Sending this two or three days ahead gives guests time to read it before they are tired and travelling.

## Stage 3: Arrival day

> Hi [Name], the home is ready for you. [Access reminder]. If anything is unclear when you arrive, message me here and I will help.

If they are arriving late:

> No problem at all. [Access method] works any time, and I will leave a light on for you. Safe travels.

## Stage 4: The first morning

> Good morning, [Name]. I hope you settled in well. Is there anything missing or anything I can help with?

This one message catches small problems early, such as a missing towel or a confusing shower, before they become a disappointing stay.

## Stage 5: When something goes wrong

Things break. A calm, quick reply matters more than a perfect one.

> I am sorry about that, and thank you for telling me. I will [fix it / bring a replacement] by [time]. If it becomes urgent, please call me on [number].

Three parts: acknowledge it, say what you will do and when, and give a way to reach you.

## Stage 6: Checkout

> Hi [Name], just a reminder that checkout is at [time] tomorrow. Before you go, please [one to three simple tasks]. Thank you for being such easy guests.

Keep checkout tasks short. A long list reads like a chore chart.

## Stage 7: After they leave

> Thank you for staying, [Name]. I hope the trip went well. If you enjoyed your stay, a short review would mean a lot. You are welcome back any time.

## Keeping it all organised

A few habits make templates genuinely useful:

1. **Store them where you reply.** Most platforms and phones let you save quick replies. Use the same headings as above so you can find them fast.
2. **Decide your policies once.** Late checkout, extra guests, early arrival: write your answer down once, then paste it when asked. You will be consistent and you will not have to think about it under pressure.
3. **Add one personal line.** A template plus one sentence about their trip ("Hope the concert is great") feels personal without taking extra time.
4. **Review after each stay.** If a guest asked something your messages did not cover, add it to your pre-arrival note.
