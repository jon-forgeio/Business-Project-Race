# A Trade Deadline Rules Template for Friendly Fantasy Leagues

Trades are where a fantasy league feels most alive. They are also where it is most likely to crack. A lopsided deal in week three is a story people laugh about. A lopsided deal between two eliminated teams the day before the deadline is a group chat on fire.

You cannot prevent every bad trade, and you should not try. What you can do is write rules that make the process predictable, so a result people dislike is still a result they accept. Below is a template you can paste into your league rules, followed by notes on each choice.

## The template

```
TRADES

T1. Review window: Accepted trades process after ____ hours.
T2. Who reviews: ____ (commissioner / league vote / no review).
T3. Reversal standard: A trade is reversed only for ____.
T4. Explanation: Any reversal is explained in the league chat within ____ hours.
T5. Deadline: No trades accepted after ____ (day, time, time zone).
T6. Pending at deadline: Trades accepted before the deadline but still in review ____.
T7. Eliminated teams: Teams mathematically out of the playoffs ____ trade.
T8. Future considerations: Trades involving draft picks or "favors" are ____.
T9. Trade-back window: The same two players cannot be traded back between the same two teams for ____ weeks.
T10. Commissioner trades: Trades involving the commissioner are reviewed by ____.
```

Fill each blank, post it, and get a quick "read it" from every manager before the season starts.

## Notes on each rule

### T1: Review window

A review window gives people time to raise a concern without freezing the league. Twenty-four hours is a common middle ground. Shorter windows feel snappy but give little time to notice a problem; longer windows can leave traded players in limbo across a lineup lock.

### T2: Who reviews

There are three common setups:

- **Commissioner review.** Fast and simple, but it puts weight on one person.
- **League vote.** Feels democratic, but rivals can block trades out of spite, and many leagues find votes turn into popularity contests.
- **No review.** Every accepted trade stands. Works well in leagues with long-standing trust.

Pick one and write it down. Mixing them ("the commissioner reviews, unless people complain, then we vote") is how disputes start.

### T3: Reversal standard

This is the most important line in the template. A narrow standard is easier to defend. Many friendly leagues use something like:

> A trade is reversed only for clear collusion: an agreement to help one team at the expense of the league, such as sharing winnings or trading players back later.

Notice what is not on that list: "I think it is unfair" or "one side is getting fleeced." Bad judgment is part of the game.

### T4: Explanation

If a trade gets reversed, the people involved deserve a reason in writing. Requiring an explanation also keeps the reviewer honest.

### T5: Deadline

Write the day, the time, and the time zone. "Week 10" is not a deadline; "Wednesday of week 10 at 11:59 p.m. Eastern" is. Many platforms have a built-in deadline setting. If yours does, make the written rule match the setting exactly.

### T6: Pending at deadline

A trade accepted five minutes before the deadline will still be in review afterward. Decide now whether it goes through. The usual answer is yes: what counts is when it was accepted.

### T7: Eliminated teams

Some leagues block eliminated teams from trading at all. Others allow it but apply extra review. If you have had problems with late-season dumps, this is the rule that addresses them.

### T8: Future considerations

Trading next year's draft picks only makes sense in keeper or dynasty leagues. In redraft leagues, "I'll owe you one" deals are hard to enforce and easy to argue about. Many commissioners simply ban them.

### T9: Trade-back window

This stops the classic "rent a player for one week" move. Four weeks is a reasonable default, or for the rest of the season if you want to be strict.

### T10: Commissioner trades

The commissioner is a manager too. Name a co-commissioner or a small panel to review trades the commissioner is part of, and say so in the rules.

## Announcing the deadline

A rule nobody remembers does not help. Post reminders:

- One week before the deadline.
- Twenty-four hours before.
- At the deadline, with "trades are now closed."

That last post matters more than it seems. It turns the deadline from a setting into an event.
