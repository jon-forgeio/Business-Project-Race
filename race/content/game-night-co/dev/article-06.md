# Fantasy Scoring Rules, Explained Simply for Your League

Scoring settings are where new managers get lost first. They see a long screen of numbers, decimals, and abbreviations, nod politely, and then ask in week four why their player scored less than they expected.

You do not need to explain every setting. You need to explain the few that change decisions, in plain words, with a worked example. Here is a method that works for any sport and any platform.

## Step 1: Find the settings that change decisions

Open your platform's scoring page and ask of each line: "Would knowing this change who someone starts or drafts?"

Most settings fail that test. A handful pass. In fantasy football, for example, the usual decision-changers are:

- Points per reception, and how much.
- Points for passing touchdowns.
- Bonuses for big games, if any.
- Negative points, such as for turnovers.
- Any custom or unusual setting your league added.

Other sports have their own short list. Whatever the sport, aim for no more than five or six items.

## Step 2: Write each one as a plain sentence

Translate the settings screen into sentences someone could read aloud.

Instead of "REC: 0.5," write:

> Each catch is worth half a point, on top of the yards and touchdowns.

Instead of "PaTD: 4," write:

> A passing touchdown is worth 4 points. A rushing or receiving touchdown is worth 6.

If your league uses a setting that differs from the platform default, say so explicitly. "We changed this from the default" is the most useful phrase in a scoring explainer.

## Step 3: Add one worked example

People learn scoring by seeing a total come together. Make up a simple, clearly hypothetical stat line and score it step by step:

> **Example (made-up stat line):** A receiver has 5 catches, 80 yards, and 1 touchdown.
> - Catches: 5 x 0.5 = 2.5
> - Yards: 80 yards at 1 point per 10 yards = 8
> - Touchdown: 6
> - **Total: 16.5 points**

Label the example as made up so nobody thinks it refers to a real player or game. One example is usually enough. Two at most.

## Step 4: Explain tiebreakers before they matter

Ties happen at two levels, and both need a written answer.

**Weekly matchup ties.** Does a tie stand, or is it broken by something like bench points or the highest-scoring starter? Many platforms allow ties by default. Decide whether that is fine for your league.

**Standings ties.** When two teams finish with the same record, what decides the higher seed? Common options, in order:

1. Total points scored for the season.
2. Head-to-head record.
3. Points scored against.
4. A coin flip or random draw.

Write your order down as a numbered list. Numbered tiebreakers are much harder to argue with than "we'll figure it out."

## Step 5: Explain stat corrections

Real sports leagues sometimes correct official stats days after a game. Most fantasy platforms apply these automatically, which can change a matchup result after people thought it was settled.

Pick one rule and post it:

- **Corrections always apply.** Results can change until the platform finalizes the week.
- **Results lock at a set time.** For example, the matchup is final once the next week's first game starts.

Either is fine. The second one prevents the "I won on Monday and lost on Thursday" message.

## Step 6: Put it on one short card

Your finished explainer should fit on one screen:

```
HOW SCORING WORKS IN OUR LEAGUE

Decision-changers:
1. ____
2. ____
3. ____
Changed from platform default: ____

Example (made-up): ____ = ____ points

Weekly tie: ____
Standings tiebreakers: 1) ____ 2) ____ 3) ____
Stat corrections: ____
```

Pin it with your rules. Point new managers to it before the draft, when scoring actually affects their choices.

## Step 7: Revisit after the season

At the end of the year, ask: did any scoring setting cause confusion or feel off? If so, add it to your list of changes for next season, and update the card. Small yearly tweaks keep the league fresh without overwhelming anyone.
