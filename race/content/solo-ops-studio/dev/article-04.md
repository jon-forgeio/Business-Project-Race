# A Before-and-After Structure for a Product Listing Description

A listing description has one job: answer the buyer's questions before they have to message you. Most descriptions do answer them, eventually, somewhere in a paragraph about the maker's inspiration. The fix is not better prose. It is a fixed structure that puts answers in the order buyers look for them.

This article gives that structure as six blocks, then applies it to a worked example. The example product is made up for illustration; swap in your own.

## The six-block structure

1. **Opening two lines:** what it is, size or quantity, and the main reason to choose it.
2. **What you get:** a short bulleted list of exactly what arrives.
3. **Specs:** dimensions, materials or file formats, weight, care.
4. **How it works:** delivery, personalisation steps, or how to download.
5. **What is not included:** frames, batteries, printing, anything a buyer might assume.
6. **Policies in brief:** processing time, returns or exchanges, and where to ask questions.

The order matters. The first two lines often show in search previews and at the top of the page, so they should stand on their own. Blocks 2 to 4 answer "what exactly am I buying?". Blocks 5 and 6 prevent the messages and disputes you would rather not have.

## Before

Here is a typical description for a fictional printable meal planner:

> I designed this planner after years of struggling to get organised in the kitchen! It's my favourite thing I've ever made and I hope it brings you as much joy as it brings me. Use it every week to plan your meals and make shopping easier. It comes in lots of sizes so you can print at home. Please note this is a digital download and nothing will be shipped. Message me with any questions! Colours may vary depending on your printer.

Nothing in it is wrong. But a buyer scanning for the page size has to read the whole thing, and still does not learn how many pages there are, which file types are included, or whether there is an editable version.

## After

> **Printable weekly meal planner, 3 pages, US Letter and A4 PDF. Plan seven days of meals and build a shopping list on one sheet.**
>
> **What you get**
> - Weekly meal grid (breakfast, lunch, dinner, snacks)
> - Shopping list sorted by aisle
> - Pantry inventory page
>
> **Specs**
> - Formats: PDF in US Letter (8.5 x 11 in) and A4
> - Pages: 3, each also included as a separate file
> - Print in colour or black and white
>
> **How it works**
> 1. Purchase and open the download link from your order page.
> 2. Download the zip and open the PDF for your paper size.
> 3. Print at home or at a print shop. Set scaling to "actual size".
>
> **Not included**
> - Physical item. Nothing is shipped.
> - Editable text fields. The pages are designed to be filled in by hand.
>
> **Good to know**
> - Printer colours vary slightly from screen colours.
> - Digital files cannot be returned, but if a file will not open, message me and I will resend it or sort it out.
> - Personal use only.

Same facts as before, plus the details that were missing, laid out so each one can be found in a second or two.

## How to do the rewrite on your own listing

1. **Extract the facts.** Read your current description and copy every factual claim into a scratch list. Ignore the tone.
2. **Fill the gaps.** Go through the six blocks and note which have no facts yet. Those gaps are what buyers end up messaging about. Fill them in.
3. **Sort into blocks.** Move each fact into its block. Delete anything repeated.
4. **Write the opening two lines last.** Once everything else is in place, it is easier to pick the one or two facts that matter most.
5. **Check against the photos and title.** Every claim should match what the images show and what the title says.
6. **Keep a short story if you want one.** If the origin of the product matters to your buyers, put two sentences about it after block 6. It is still there for anyone who wants it, without blocking the answers.

## A quick test

Give the new description to someone who has never seen the product and ask them three questions: how big is it, what exactly arrives, and what happens if something goes wrong? If they can answer all three without scrolling back up, the structure is working.

You can paste this skeleton into a notes app and reuse it for every listing:

```
[Line 1: what it is, size/quantity]
[Line 2: main reason to choose it]

-
How it works
1.
Not included
-

-
```
