# How to Rewrite an Etsy Listing Title and 13 Tags, Step by Step

Most listing titles are written from the maker's side. They use the product's nickname, the collection name, or a phrase that sounds nice. Buyers search from the other side. They type what the thing is and what they need it for. Rewriting a title is mostly the work of moving from your words to theirs.

This is a method you can run on one listing in about fifteen minutes. It does not promise any change in how a listing is shown in search; it just makes the title and tags clearer and more accurate, which is the part you control.

## Step 1: Write down the listing as it is

Copy the current title and all current tags into a scratch file. Number the tags. This gives you something to compare against and lets you undo the change cleanly if you want to.

## Step 2: Describe the item in plain words

Without looking at the current title, write one sentence answering: what is it, what is it made of or what format is it, and who is it for?

Example: "A hand-stitched leather card holder that fits four cards, for someone who wants a slim wallet."

That sentence is your raw material. Your current title may share almost no words with it, which is a sign the rewrite is worth doing.

## Step 3: List the phrases a buyer would type

From the sentence, pull out short phrases a person might put into a search box. Aim for ten to fifteen. For the card holder:

- leather card holder
- slim wallet
- minimalist wallet
- front pocket wallet
- card case
- hand stitched leather
- gift for him
- anniversary gift leather
- small leather goods

Start typing a couple of these into the marketplace search bar and note the suggested endings that appear. Those suggestions show you how other people phrase the same idea. Add any that fit your item honestly.

## Step 4: Build the title in a fixed order

Use this order every time:

1. **What it is**, in the most common phrasing from your list.
2. **The key attribute**: material, size, format or capacity.
3. **Who it is for or the occasion.**

Before: `The Wanderer | Handmade Card Sleeve`

After: `Leather Card Holder, Slim Minimalist Wallet for 4 Cards, Hand Stitched Gift for Him`

Read it out loud. If it sounds like a keyword pile rather than a description, cut words until it reads naturally. Remove repeated words; each one only needs to appear once. Put your collection or brand name at the end only if there is room.

## Step 5: Fill all 13 tags with a mix

Tags are short phrases, not single words, and each slot can hold one idea. A simple split that keeps you from repeating yourself:

- **5 product phrases**: leather card holder, slim wallet, minimalist wallet, card case, front pocket wallet
- **3 material or style phrases**: hand stitched leather, full grain leather, small leather goods
- **3 buyer or occasion phrases**: gift for him, anniversary gift, groomsmen gift
- **2 use or format phrases**: everyday carry, travel card case

Check each tag against three questions:

- Would a real buyer type this?
- Is it true for this item?
- Is it different from every other tag on the list?

Drop anything that fails one of them. Copying tags from unrelated popular listings tends to bring the wrong visitors, which helps nobody.

## Step 6: Match the first two description lines

The opening lines of your description should confirm the title: what it is, the size or quantity, and the main reason to choose it. If your title says "fits 4 cards", the first lines should say so too. A mismatch between title, photos and description is the fastest way to lose a buyer's trust.

## Step 7: Log and wait

Write the date, the old title and the new title in a sheet. Leave the listing alone for at least two weeks before judging it, and change nothing else on it in the meantime, so you can tell whether the rewrite made a difference in your own numbers.

## A compact checklist

```
[ ] Copy old title + tags to a scratch file
[ ] One-sentence plain description
[ ] 10-15 buyer phrases, checked against search suggestions
[ ] Title: what it is > key attribute > who/occasion
[ ] Read aloud, remove repeats
[ ] 13 tags: 5 product, 3 material/style, 3 buyer/occasion, 2 use/format
[ ] First two description lines match the title
[ ] Log the change with a date
```
