# A Weekly Shop Maintenance Checklist You Can Run in 30 Minutes

A solo shop has no ops team. Nobody else notices that a listing photo is out of date, that a message has sat for three days, or that a product has quietly stopped getting views. The fix is not more effort. It is a short, fixed checklist that runs at the same time every week, whether or not you feel like it.

This is the checklist I would hand to anyone starting a small online shop. It is split into five blocks with rough time boxes, so it fits in half an hour.

## Pick a slot and protect it

Choose one day and time, put it in your calendar as a recurring event, and give it a name you will not ignore, such as "Shop maintenance". Monday morning works for many sellers because it catches anything that came in over the weekend. The specific day matters less than keeping it the same.

## Block 1: Inbox and reviews (8 minutes)

- Open every unread message. Answer what you can in one pass.
- For anything that needs research, reply with a short holding note and a date you will follow up.
- Read new reviews. Reply to each in one sentence, thanking the buyer and, if relevant, answering any question in it.
- If a review mentions a problem, add it to your change log (see Block 5) as a possible fix.

The rule here is no message older than the end of this block. If you clear the inbox every week, it never becomes a weekend project.

## Block 2: Numbers glance (5 minutes)

You are not doing analysis. You are noting a few facts so you can compare later.

- Write down the week's views, visits and orders from your shop dashboard.
- Note your three listings with the most views.
- Note your three listings with the weakest conversion (views with no orders).

Copy these into a simple sheet with the date in the first column. After a month you will have a view of what is moving, based on your own shop rather than guesses.

## Block 3: One listing improvement (10 minutes)

Pick one listing from your weakest three and change one thing. Only one:

- the first image,
- the title,
- the tags,
- or the first two lines of the description.

Changing one element at a time is the only way to have any idea what helped. If you rewrite the title, swap the photo and change the price in the same week, you learn nothing from the result.

## Block 4: Housekeeping (4 minutes)

- Check stock levels or file links on your top three listings.
- Confirm shop announcements and holiday notices are still accurate.
- Check that your shipping profiles or delivery settings match what your listings promise.
- Delete or archive anything you have meant to retire for more than a month.

These are small items that go stale slowly. Four minutes a week stops them from piling up.

## Block 5: Log and close (3 minutes)

Keep a plain change log, either in a notes app or a sheet with four columns:

| Date | Listing | What changed | Check again on |
|------|---------|--------------|----------------|
| 2026-12-01 | Linen napkin set | New first image, natural light | 2026-12-15 |

Set the "check again on" date two weeks out. When that date arrives, compare the listing's numbers in your weekly sheet before and after. Keep the change, revert it, or try a different element.

Then close the shop dashboard. The checklist is done for the week.

## A printable version

Here is the whole thing as a block you can paste into any notes app:

```
WEEKLY SHOP MAINTENANCE (30 min)
[ ] 1. Inbox: answer or hold every message (8)
[ ] 1b. Reviews: reply to each in one sentence
[ ] 2. Numbers: views, visits, orders; top 3 views; bottom 3 conversion (5)
[ ] 3. Change ONE thing on ONE weak listing (10)
[ ] 4. Stock/files, announcements, shipping settings, retire list (4)
[ ] 5. Log the change, set a check date 2 weeks out (3)
```

## Common ways this breaks

- **Skipping the log.** Without dates, two weeks later you will not remember what you changed.
- **Turning Block 3 into a full afternoon.** If a listing needs a full rewrite, schedule it separately.
- **Running it only when sales are slow.** The point is to run it every week, including good ones.
