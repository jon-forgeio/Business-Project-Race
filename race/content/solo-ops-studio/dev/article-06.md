# How to Write a Project Kickoff Checklist You Will Actually Use

A kickoff is the cheapest moment in a project to prevent problems. Everyone is paying attention, nothing has been built yet, and a missing answer costs a quick question instead of a redo. Yet many freelancers run kickoffs from memory, and the same items get skipped: who approves, what is out of scope, when feedback is due.

A kickoff checklist fixes that. This article shows how to write one in three phases, gives a starting version, and explains how to keep it short enough that you use it on every project.

## Principle: a checklist is for the steps you skip

A good checklist is not a full manual. It lists the items you know how to do but tend to forget under time pressure. If you never forget to send a calendar invite, leave it off. If you always forget to ask who signs off, put it near the top.

To find those items, look back at your last few projects and write down every problem that could have been caught at the start. Each one becomes a line.

## Phase 1: Before the call

These items happen once the client says yes, before you meet.

- [ ] Intake questions sent and returned.
- [ ] Intake answers read; open questions listed for the call.
- [ ] Scope summary drafted from the intake (deliverables, revisions, exclusions).
- [ ] Draft schedule with key dates.
- [ ] Agenda sent with the call invite.
- [ ] List of assets and access you will need, with who provides each.

Sending the scope summary and schedule before the call means the meeting is spent confirming and correcting, not drafting from scratch.

## Phase 2: During the call

Use a fixed agenda so nothing depends on how the conversation flows. A 30-minute version:

1. **Goal (5 min).** Read back the problem and the desired result in one sentence each. Ask the client to correct them.
2. **Scope (10 min).** Walk through deliverables, revisions and exclusions. Read the exclusions out loud.
3. **People (5 min).** Who gives feedback, who makes the final decision, and who else needs to see work before it is approved.
4. **Schedule (5 min).** Confirm each date and the feedback window. Agree what happens if feedback is late.
5. **Next steps (5 min).** List what each side does next and by when.

The checklist items for this phase:

- [ ] Goal and result confirmed in the client's words.
- [ ] Exclusions read aloud and agreed.
- [ ] Final decision-maker named.
- [ ] Feedback channel agreed (one place, not several).
- [ ] Dates and late-feedback rule confirmed.
- [ ] Next actions listed with owners and dates.

Naming the final decision-maker is the item most worth protecting. Projects stall when feedback arrives from someone who was never in the loop.

## Phase 3: After the call

These items close the loop the same day.

- [ ] Recap email sent within 24 hours: goal, scope, people, dates, next steps.
- [ ] Scope summary and schedule updated with changes from the call.
- [ ] Recurring status update added to your calendar.
- [ ] Project folder created and shared.
- [ ] Access requests sent, through each tool's own sharing feature.
- [ ] First invoice sent, if your terms include a deposit.

The recap email is the most important item on the list. It turns a conversation into a written record that both sides can refer back to.

## Keep it to one page

Your checklist should fit on one screen. If it grows past twenty items, look for lines that are always done without being prompted and remove them. A checklist that is too long gets skimmed, which defeats the purpose.

## Store it where you start projects

Put the checklist where you begin each project: as the first file in your project template folder, a pinned note, or a task template in your project tool. If you have to search for it, you will skip it on a busy week.

## Review it after each project

When a project ends, spend five minutes asking: what went wrong that the kickoff could have caught? Add one line for each answer. Remove lines that never caught anything. Over time the checklist reflects how you work and the clients you work with.

## The compact version

```
BEFORE
[ ] Intake returned and read
[ ] Scope summary + schedule drafted
[ ] Agenda sent; asset list ready
DURING
[ ] Goal confirmed in client's words
[ ] Exclusions read aloud
[ ] Final decision-maker named
[ ] One feedback channel agreed
[ ] Dates + late-feedback rule confirmed
AFTER
[ ] Recap email within 24h
[ ] Docs updated; status reminder set
[ ] Folder shared; access requested
```

The recap email after the call follows the same pattern as a kickoff email.
