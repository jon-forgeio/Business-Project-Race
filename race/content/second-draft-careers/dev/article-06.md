# How to Track Job Applications Without Losing Your Mind

A few weeks into a job search, the details start to blur. Which version of your resume did you send to that company? Did the recruiter say they would call back on Tuesday or next Tuesday? Have you already messaged this person?

A tracker fixes that. It does not need to be fancy. A spreadsheet, a notes table or a simple board all work. What matters is that you record the same few things every time and look at them once a week.

## Why track at all

- **You stop double-messaging people** or forgetting who you talked to.
- **Follow-ups happen on time**, because the date is written down.
- **You can see patterns**, such as which sources lead to replies and which do not.
- **Interviews go better**, because you can quickly check what you sent and why you were interested.

## The columns that matter

Start with these. You can add more later, but resist the urge to build a dashboard on day one.

| Column | What to write |
|---|---|
| Company | The organisation name |
| Role | The exact job title from the posting |
| Link | Where the posting lives, so you can reread it |
| Source | Referral, recruiter, job board, company site, event |
| Contact | The person you spoke to or applied through, if any |
| Date applied | When you sent it |
| Resume version | A short label, such as "ops-v2" |
| Status | Applied, replied, screening, interview, offer, closed |
| Next step | The very next action |
| Next date | When to take that action |
| Notes | Anything you will want to remember later |

The two most important columns are **Next step** and **Next date**. A row without a next step is a row you will forget.

## Keep the status list short

Too many status options make the tracker harder to update. A short list is enough:

- **Applied**: sent, no response yet.
- **In conversation**: any reply, screening call or recruiter contact.
- **Interviewing**: a scheduled or completed interview.
- **Offer**: an offer in hand.
- **Closed**: rejected, withdrawn, or no reply after your last follow-up.

"Closed" is not failure. It just means the row no longer needs your attention.

## Label your resume versions

If you tailor your resume for different roles (and it usually helps to), save each version with a clear name and record which one you sent. Before an interview, open that exact version so you remember what the interviewer has read.

A simple naming habit: `lastname-role-v1`, `lastname-role-v2`.

## Set simple follow-up rules

Decide your rules once so you do not have to think about them every time. For example:

- **After applying with no reply:** one short follow-up after about a week, if you have a contact.
- **After a call or interview:** a thank-you note the same day or the next.
- **After a recruiter says "we will be in touch":** if the date they mentioned passes, a polite check-in a couple of days later.
- **After your last follow-up with no reply:** mark it closed and move on.

Write the next follow-up date in the tracker the moment you send something.

## A weekly review in fifteen minutes

Once a week, sort the tracker by **Next date** and work through it:

1. Send any follow-ups that are due.
2. Close rows that have gone quiet past your last follow-up.
3. Update statuses from the past week.
4. Look at the Source column. Which sources led to conversations? Which led to nothing?
5. Decide one thing to do differently next week.

That last step is where the tracker earns its keep. If referrals keep leading to calls and job boards do not, you know where to put more time.

## Track conversations too, not only applications

Many good leads start as conversations rather than applications. Add a second tab or a section for networking:

- Name
- Company
- How you know them
- Date contacted
- What you asked
- Reply
- Next step and date

Over time, this becomes a record of the people who helped you, which is useful when you want to thank them later.

## Keep it private and tidy

Your tracker may hold names and notes about real people. Keep it somewhere private, avoid writing anything you would not want them to read, and delete rows you no longer need once the search is over.
