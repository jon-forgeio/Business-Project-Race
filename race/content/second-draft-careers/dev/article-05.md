# A 30-Day Job Search Plan, One Week at a Time

A job search without structure tends to swing between bursts of frantic applying and days of not knowing what to do next. A simple weekly rhythm helps. It gives each week a focus, keeps the work to a sustainable amount, and makes it easier to see what is working.

This plan covers about thirty days in four weeks. Adjust the amounts to fit your energy, your finances and any other responsibilities. The structure matters more than the exact numbers.

## The weekly rhythm

Every week, regardless of focus, has the same skeleton:

- **Monday: plan.** Decide this week's targets. Write them down.
- **Tuesday to Thursday: do the work.** Applications, messages, practice, calls.
- **Friday: follow up and tidy.** Send follow-ups, update your tracker.
- **Weekend: rest**, or one light task if you want it.

Blocking time this way keeps the search from swallowing every hour of every day.

## Week 1: Build the base

The goal is not volume. It is having good materials and a clear target.

- Write down your recent wins: what you did, what changed, and any honest numbers.
- Rewrite your resume bullets using the pattern verb, what, how much, result.
- Update your LinkedIn headline so it says what you do and who you help.
- Decide on one or two target role titles. Being specific makes everything else easier.
- Draft a short list of companies you would like to work for.
- Set up a simple tracker (a spreadsheet or notes table is enough).

By the end of the week you should have an accurate resume, a clear target and a list to work from.

## Week 2: Start conversations

People who already know your work are often the warmest starting point.

- Message former colleagues and managers with a short, specific note about what you are looking for.
- Reach out to a few recruiters who work in your field.
- Contact a couple of people at companies on your list. Ask small questions, not for referrals.
- Book a few short calls. Prepare two or three questions for each.
- Begin applying to roles where you already have a connection.

Keep each message brief and easy to answer. A single small ask per message works best.

## Week 3: Apply with focus

Now you have materials, a target and some conversations going. Time to apply steadily.

- Choose a manageable number of applications for the week, and favour quality over count.
- For each role, adjust your resume so the most relevant bullets come first.
- Write a short note for each application that links one of your results to one need in the posting.
- Practise common interview questions out loud. Record yourself and replay the weakest answer.
- Keep following up on Week 2 conversations.

Tailored applications take longer, but they are much easier to talk about in an interview.

## Week 4: Review and adjust

Look back before you push forward.

Open your tracker and ask:

- Which sources led to replies or calls: referrals, recruiters, direct applications, job boards?
- Which kinds of roles got a response, and which got silence?
- Where did you spend time that produced nothing?
- Which interview questions felt hardest?

Then set next month's plan based on what you learned. If direct applications are getting no replies but conversations are, shift more time to conversations. If one role type gets interest and another does not, narrow your target.

## A few habits that help across all four weeks

- **Keep a wins file.** One line a week about anything you did, learned or finished, even during the search.
- **Protect one rest day.** Searching every day tends to wear people down without producing more.
- **Write down every follow-up date.** It is easy to forget who you promised to get back to.
- **Celebrate inputs, not only outcomes.** You control how many good messages you send. You do not control when someone replies.

## When the plan slips

Some weeks will go sideways. A family thing, a bad day, a role you were excited about falling through. That is normal. Pick up at the start of the next week rather than trying to make up every missed task at once.
