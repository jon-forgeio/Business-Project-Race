# Second Draft Careers: Mastodon posts

1. A resume bullet that starts with "Responsible for" describes the job, not you. Try: verb, what you worked on, what changed. "Rebuilt the weekly schedule so the team stopped double-booking" says more than any duty list. Five worked rewrites here: https://jon-forgeio.github.io/Business-Project-Race/second-draft-careers/ #resume #jobsearch

2. No exact number for a resume bullet? Do not guess a percentage. Use a count you know for sure (people trained, documents written) or a clear before and after: "from an inbox with no owner to a tracked queue." Honest and specific beats impressive and vague. #resume #career

3. First day after a layoff: before any job hunting, write down your last day, severance terms and benefits end dates. Put every deadline in a calendar. A short checklist for days one and two: https://jon-forgeio.github.io/Business-Project-Race/second-draft-careers/ #layoffs #jobsearch

4. Your memory of your last job is sharpest in the first few days after it ends. Spend an hour listing ten things you did and what changed because of each. That list becomes your resume bullets and your interview stories. #jobsearch #career

5. Week one after a job loss is not the week to apply everywhere. Stabilise, write down your wins, update the resume, and message a handful of people who already know your work. Applications land better once that base is in place. #layoffs #jobsearch

6. Your LinkedIn headline shows next to your name in search, comments and messages. "Marketing Specialist" says what you are. "Marketing specialist helping small shops turn browsers into buyers" says who you help. Free guide: https://jon-forgeio.github.io/Business-Project-Race/second-draft-careers/ #linkedin #career

7. Only the first lines of your LinkedIn About section show before "see more." Open with who you help and with what. Save the backstory for later paragraphs, or cut it. #linkedin #career

8. Before sending a LinkedIn message, finish this sentence: "I am writing to this person because..." If the answer is "to grow my network," wait until you have a reason they can act on. #linkedin #networking

9. A networking message that gets answered usually has three sentences: why you are writing, one relevant fact about you, and one small question with a clear yes or no. One ask per message. #networking #jobsearch

10. Quick test for any outreach message: could you send it, unchanged, to ten other people? If yes, add one detail that only fits this person. That single line is often the difference between a reply and silence. #networking #career

11. Before pitching yourself as a podcast guest, listen to at least two episodes. Open your email with one specific moment you found useful. It shows the host you know the show. Pitch template and subject lines: https://jon-forgeio.github.io/Business-Project-Race/second-draft-careers/ #podcasting #career

12. Podcast pitch tip: frame your topic as the listener's problem, not your résumé. "Your listeners run small teams and struggle with handovers" gives a host a reason to book you. Add two or three one-line talking points so they can picture the episode. #podcasting #marketing

13. Podcast guest bios do not need your life story. One line works: "[Name] helps [audience] [result] through [method]." Plus one link. The host can ask for more if they want it. #podcasting

14. Follow-ups without pressure: one gentle nudge after about a week, one final note a week later that says "no problem if it is not a fit," then stop. Works for pitches, applications and networking messages alike. #jobsearch #career

15. Give your job search a weekly rhythm: Monday plan, Tuesday to Thursday do the work, Friday follow up and tidy, weekend rest. It keeps the search from eating every hour of every day. #jobsearch #productivity

16. A job search that starts with "apply to 150 companies" tends to stall. Start with around fifteen you would genuinely like to work for. For each, note one person you could contact and one recent thing about the company. #jobsearch

17. The two most important columns in a job application tracker: "next step" and "next date." A row without a next step is a row you will forget. #jobsearch #productivity

18. If you tailor your resume per role, name each version clearly and note which one you sent in your tracker. Before an interview, reopen that exact file so you know what the interviewer has read. #resume #jobsearch

19. Fifteen-minute weekly job search review: send due follow-ups, close rows that went quiet, then check which sources led to real conversations. Spend more time next week on whatever worked. #jobsearch #productivity

20. "Why did you leave?" after a layoff can be short and neutral: "My role was eliminated in a reorganisation. I am proud of [result], and I am looking for [what you want next]." Practise it out loud until it feels plain, not rehearsed. More first-week steps: https://jon-forgeio.github.io/Business-Project-Race/second-draft-careers/ #interviews #layoffs
