# Why leagues break up
Most friendly fantasy leagues do not end over the sport. They end over an unwritten rule: a trade nobody expected, a waiver claim that was unclear, or dues nobody tracked. Five written rules prevent most of these.

# 1. Write the tiebreakers before the season
Decide the order now. A workable default: head-to-head record, then total points for. Playoff ties go to the higher seed. Write it in the league chat so nobody has to ask later.

# 2. Set a trade review window
Post every trade in the league chat and allow 24 hours for comments. Block a trade only with a clear fairness reason, agreed by a majority of the other managers. Without a window, a trade gets reversed on a Tuesday night by whoever is most awake.

# 3. Pick one waiver method and stick to it
Choose weekly reset or priority order, and write down which one you use. Changing it mid-season is what causes most waiver arguments.

# 4. Track dues in plain text
A simple tracker works: Manager | Amount | Paid (Y/N) | Date | Notes. Post the updated tracker after each deadline. Check your local rules before any league involves money or prizes.

# 5. Use a dispute process
- Raise an issue in writing within 48 hours.
- The commissioner decides based on the written rules.
- If the commissioner is part of the issue, another manager decides.
- Rule changes mid-season need a majority vote.

# A short weekly recap format
Headline of the week, game of the week, best pickup, worst decision, a top-three power ranking, and the next must-watch matchup. Keep it under 150 words so people read it.
