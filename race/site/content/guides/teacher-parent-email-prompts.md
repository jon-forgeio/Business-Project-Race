# Privacy first
Never paste student names, ids, health or family details into an AI tool. Write "the student" or use initials, and add real details yourself after you have the draft. Follow your school or district policy on AI use. These are drafting aids, not replacements for your judgement.
# Five prompts to start with
- "Act as a primary school teacher. Write a friendly email to a parent about a student who has been struggling with homework completion. Tone: supportive and specific. Include one positive, one concern and one next step. 140 words."
- "Draft an email asking a parent to schedule a conference. Offer three time windows and a short agenda. Warm tone."
- "Write an email explaining a change in classroom routine: [change]. Plain language, include a reason and a start date."
- "Write a reply to a parent upset about a grade. Calm, acknowledge feelings, explain the grading criteria, offer a meeting."
- "Rewrite this to be warmer without being unprofessional, and keep it under 120 words: [your draft, with no names]."
# How to get better drafts
- Name the situation, the tone, and the length.
- Ask for one positive, one concern and one next step.
- Read the draft out loud. If it sounds like a form letter, edit it.
- Check every fact against your own records before sending.
