# The rule before you pitch
Listen to at least two episodes first. A pitch that mentions a specific moment from the show is read. A pitch that could go to any show is deleted.
# What a good pitch contains
- One specific detail from an episode, so the host knows you listened.
- A topic framed as a problem the audience has, not a description of you.
- Two or three talking points, so the host can picture the conversation.
- A one-line credential, not a biography.
- One easy next step.
# A pitch to adapt
"Hi [Host], I enjoyed your episode on [episode], especially [one specific detail]. I work on [your area] and could speak about [topic]: 1) [talking point], 2) [talking point], 3) [talking point]. Here is a one-line bio: [bio]. If it fits your show, I would be glad to share more. Thanks for the great work."
# Subject lines that get opened
- Guest idea for [Show]: [topic] in [number] minutes
- A fresh angle on [topic] for your listeners
- What [audience] gets wrong about [topic]
- Loved your episode on [topic]; a related idea
# Follow-ups
- Day 7: one short nudge offering a brief outline.
- Day 14: one last note that makes it easy to say no.
- After that, stop. Send something useful in a month with no ask.
