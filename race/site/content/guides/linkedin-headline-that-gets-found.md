# Why most headlines fail
A headline that repeats your job title tells a reader what you are, not who you help or what problem you solve. Search matches words in the headline, so it has to include the words your target readers type.

# The headline formula
- Who you help, with the role they hire for.
- The result or problem you work on.
- One credential or specific skill, if you have one.
Keep it under about 120 characters so it shows fully on a phone.

# The About section structure
1. First two lines: the reader's problem, in their words. These show before "see more."
2. Three or four short paragraphs: what you do, one concrete example, and how you work.
3. A closing line that says what you want the reader to do next.

# Before and after (illustrative example, not a real profile)
- Before: "Marketing Specialist | Passionate about growth | Team player"
  After: "Email marketing for B2B software teams | Turning trial users into paying customers | Lifecycle campaigns, HubSpot"
- Before About opening: "I am a dedicated professional with many years of experience."
  After About opening: "Software trials stall when nobody follows up in the first 72 hours. I build the emails that do."

# A ten-minute check
- Would a recruiter know your target role from the headline alone?
- Are there words in the headline a buyer or recruiter would search?
- Is every claim something you can explain in an interview?
- Does the About section avoid empty words like "passionate" and "dedicated"?
