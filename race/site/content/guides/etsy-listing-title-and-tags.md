# Start with the buyer's words
Before you write anything, list five phrases a buyer would type into search. Use the words they use, not your internal product name. "Printable habit tracker" usually beats "Minimalist wellness planner insert."

# The title (first 40 characters matter most)
1. Put the main keyword in the first 40 characters: what it is, then who it is for.
2. Add one or two details a buyer filters on: format, count, or use.
3. Keep it readable. A title that reads like a list of tags is a warning sign to buyers.

# The 13 tags
- 5 exact phrases a buyer would type.
- 4 use cases (for example, "gift for teacher").
- 2 occasions.
- 2 formats or sizes.
Do not repeat a word you already used in the title in every tag. Vary the phrasing.

# Before and after (illustrative example, not a real listing)
- Before: "Cute Planner Sheet for Busy Lives"
  After: "Printable Weekly Meal Planner, Letter and A4 PDF, Family Grocery List"
  Why: The format and the buyer's search words come first. The "cute" and "busy lives" phrases are vague and rarely searched.

# A quick self-check
- Could a stranger tell what they are buying from the first 40 characters?
- Does each tag add a new search phrase, not a repeat?
- Would the description answer the first question a buyer has?
