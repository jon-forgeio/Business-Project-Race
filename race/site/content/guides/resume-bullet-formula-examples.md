# The problem with most bullets
"Responsible for customer support" tells a hiring manager what you were assigned, not what you did. Most weak bullets name a duty and stop there. A stronger bullet gives an action, a specific detail, and a result.

# The three-part formula
1. Action: start with a strong verb you can defend in an interview (cut, built, rebuilt, negotiated, trained).
2. Specifics: what you worked on, with a number if you have one (volume, time, count, team size).
3. Result: what changed because you did it (time saved, errors reduced, revenue, a deadline met).

# Five before and after examples
These are illustrative examples written for this guide, not from a real person's resume. Replace the details with your own facts.

- Before: "Responsible for scheduling shifts for the team."
  After: "Rebuilt weekly shift scheduling for a 14-person team, cutting schedule conflicts from about 6 per week to 1."
- Before: "Helped customers with billing questions."
  After: "Resolved 40 to 50 billing tickets per week, and wrote a three-step reply template that brought first-reply time under 2 hours."
- Before: "Worked on the monthly sales report."
  After: "Automated the monthly sales report in a spreadsheet, saving about 4 hours of manual copying each month."
- Before: "Managed social media accounts."
  After: "Ran posting for two accounts with 8,000 combined followers, growing engagement by about 30 percent over six months."
- Before: "Involved in a project to move the warehouse system."
  After: "Tested 120 product records during a warehouse system move, finding and fixing errors before go-live."

# A ten-minute check for your own bullets
- Does it start with a specific verb, not "responsible for" or "helped"?
- Is there a number, a named tool, or a named group of people?
- Can you say what changed because of it?
- Would you be able to explain it for two minutes if asked?
- If you cannot answer one of these, ask yourself what you actually did, and write that instead.
