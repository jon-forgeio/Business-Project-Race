Most wedding toasts run long because the speaker starts with the story they remember best, not the one that fits the room. This outline works in the other order: decide the length first, then pick the story.

## Step 1: Set the length
A toast that is heard well runs about 2 to 3 minutes, which is roughly 300 to 400 words. Written text reads faster than it is spoken, so count words in a draft and expect to cut a third after you read it aloud.

## Step 2: Pick one story
Write one sentence for each of these and keep the one that makes you smile while you write it:
- A moment you first saw how the couple acts around each other.
- A small, specific habit that everyone in the room will recognise.
- A time one of them surprised you by being kinder or braver than expected.
- Something that went wrong and turned out fine.

If your story needs more than 40 seconds to tell, it is too big for a toast. Cut it.

## Step 3: Fill a structure
Each structure has the same three parts: an opener that gets a laugh or a warm silence, the story, and a closer that raises the glass.

- Best man or close friend: open with a light joke at your own expense, tell the story, then wish them a specific thing, not a generic one.
- Maid or matron of honour: open with how you met, keep the story to one moment, close by naming what you admire about how they treat each other.
- Parent: open with a memory from early childhood, tell the story from the parent's point of view, close with a line about what you hope they build together.
- Friend (not in the wedding party): keep it shorter, focus on your friendship with one of them, and say how you have seen them change.

## Step 4: Read it out loud twice
Read it standing up, once to yourself and once to a friend. Mark any sentence you run out of breath on and split it. Replace any phrase you would not say in person.

## Checklist before the day
- Under 400 words, or about 3 minutes at a steady pace.
- One story, not three.
- The couple's names said correctly.
- A printed copy with large text, in case you freeze.
- Your glass is ready before you start.
